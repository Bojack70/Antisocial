Round one · what to fix

Thirteen came back. Seven are done, four need a re-render, one needs a look, one should ship with no art, and one never rendered. The palette held across the whole set, which was the hard part — everything below is detail work.

Three decisions before you re-run anything. Each affects more than one image, so settle them first — especially the first, which decides whether the other two matter.

A

Flat set, or the rendered one?

The extra image — the woman breathing among the trees — is a different style from the other thirteen, not a fourteenth member of the set. It has soft gradients through the hills, veins drawn into the leaves, paper texture, a fully rendered face with blush, and deep forest greens rather than the muted sage in your Tailwind palette. It's also masked into an organic blob shape none of the others use. Put it beside the flat window tile and they look like two different illustrators.

It is, to be fair, the best image in the whole batch — and it's the style from your original mockup, so it may be what you actually wanted all along. But pick one lane. Flat is cheaper, holds consistency across thirty images, and matches "flat colour fills only" in your own config. Rendered is more beautiful per image, drifts more between generations, and would mean re-running the seven that are currently done and deepening the greens in tailwind.config.js.

My call: flat — but only because consistency across a long feed matters more than any single card, and seven are already there. If you'd rather have the rendered look, say so and I'll rewrite all 29 briefs for it; the current ones are written for flat.

B

Do figures have faces?

I'm reversing what I told you an hour ago. I'd said no faces, based on the two weak ones in the flat set and on my own SVG profile failing. The breathing image disproves that — the face there is genuinely good. So the problem was never faces; it was faces rendered that flat, where there's no shading to carry the features.

Which means this follows from decision A. Rendered style: faces are fine, use them. Flat style: no faces — turn figures away or crop above the shoulders, because flat is exactly the mode where they fail. The re-prompts below assume flat-and-faceless; if you pick rendered, skip the posture and own-voice re-runs since those tiles are already fine.

C

Are the source files square or 4:3?

In the contact sheet every tile looks square, and the breathing image is wider still — closer to 16:9. The cards are 4:3, so neither drops in cleanly. Check one original before re-running anything. If they're square you'll be cropping all thirteen by hand; it's cheaper to state the ratio up front. Add "wide 4:3 landscape format, 1024 by 768" to the end of every prompt from here on.

Before you run these

Four things that will save you regenerations.

  • Do one card first, not thirty-three. Run a single prompt, check the palette actually landed, then batch the rest. Gemini drifts warm-neutral toward beige-grey, and it's cheaper to catch that once.
  • Hands are the risk in the Try This set. Eleven of the sixteen skills are done with hands. The prompts below crop to fingertips or show the object mid-action without a full hand wherever the skill allows it — that's deliberate, not an oversight.
  • The "no text" instruction needs repeating. Prompts about numbers, folding, and knots pull hard toward diagram labels. If a render comes back with lettering, re-prompt with "no text" moved to the front of the sentence.
  • Ask for one more size than you need. These sit at roughly 300 × 225 pt in a card, on screens up to 3×. Generate at 1200 px wide or more so there's room to downsample cleanly.
