Can hand-drawn SVG carry the card art?

Six pieces in the paper-and-clay palette, drawn as vector paths rather than generated as images. Two of them put a human figure on the page, because that's the part I said I'd do worst — judge that claim yourself rather than take it on trust.

What you're seeing
Every illustration is SVG geometry — no PNG, no external file, nothing fetched at runtime.
Why it matters
Colours are palette tokens, so the art recolours with the theme instead of being fixed at export.
How to judge
Large plate on the left for the drawing; a true-scale card on the right for how it actually lands.
Honest ratings
Green dots mean I'd ship it. Amber means passable. Clay means I'd argue against it.
Digital Break figure · profile · 1.9 KB
The hard case

This is the closest match to your reference, and the one I'd push back on. The landscape behind her is fine — hills, light, leaves all sit right. The face is where it thins out. A profile in flat vector needs the nose, lip and chin to read in about five path segments, and mine reads as a mask rather than a person. Compare it against the Gemini render and you'll see the gap immediately: hers has soft shading through the cheek and hair, mine is one flat tone.

My read: keep the scene, lose the face. Piece 5 below shows the alternative.

Look At Something Far Away scene · no figure · 2.2 KB
Ship it

This is what flat vector is actually for. Every element is a shape a rectangle or an arc can describe honestly — frame, mullions, sill, hills — so nothing is straining to look like something it can't. It also does a job the text can't: the card says "look at something far away" and the drawing supplies the distance, which is the whole point of the exercise.

This one already has a home — it's the existing mint Gentle Reminder card in your feed.

Simple Magic object · rotated group · 1.6 KB
Strong

This replaces the photograph in your mockup — the one of hands shuffling a deck, which I can't draw and can't generate. The fan is five copies of one rectangle at different rotations, which is why it looks even: it's geometry, not draughtsmanship. That's the trick to lean on wherever a subject allows it.

The one thing lost against a photo is the human hand, and with it some of the "you could do this too" pull. Worth weighing.

What this settles, and what it doesn't

Five of the six clear the bar in my view. The one that doesn't is the one you'd most want, which is worth saying plainly rather than burying.

01

Faces are the limit, not figures. Piece 5 shows a person reading fine from behind; piece 1 shows the same person's face not quite arriving. If you want faces at your reference's quality, that's a commissioned illustrator or a generation tool — not me drawing paths.

02

Photographs stay out of reach entirely. The shuffling-hands shot in your mockup has no vector equivalent. If that specific card needs a photo, it's Unsplash or Pexels under licence, or you supply it.

03

Four of these six illustrate cards that don't exist yet. Only Gentle Reminder and The Notebook are live. Learn a Skill, Field Trip, and Daily Read are from your design board, so art for them is downstream of the scope question still open.

04

The mural idea has a home. The wall in piece 5 is the natural surface for session-end unlocks — same scene, more paint on it each time. That's a feature, not a reskin, but the art supports it now.
